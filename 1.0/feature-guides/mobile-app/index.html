    The mobile app¶

    The Olano app puts your agents on your phone: conversations with them, what Cortex is working on, the approvals and alerts waiting for you, and notifications when an agent needs you. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Android phones and tablets, and on Apple silicon Macs as the iPad app, and comes from the App Store and Google Play.

    The app is built for the phone: a tab bar along the bottom, a back button and the swipe back gesture on every screen, pull down to refresh, and the look of the platform it runs on (iOS on an iPhone, Material on Android), in light or dark (see Appearance). It talks to your own deployment, with the same agents, conversations and permissions as the dashboard. Nothing about your agents is stored by Olano on the way.

    Connecting¶

    The first screen asks for your Olano address: the address you open your dashboard at in a browser, such as acme.olano.run. A bare name like acme means acme.olano.run; a full address (https://agents.example.com) works for a deployment on its own domain. The app checks that the address answers as an Olano deployment before it continues, and says so if it does not (no such address, a website that is not Olano, no network).

    You then sign in with the email and password you use for the dashboard. The sign-in is kept on the phone for two weeks of inactivity, so you sign in again only when it expires or you sign out. A deployment with no accounts yet asks you to finish setting it up in its dashboard first. A single-user deployment (no accounts at all) opens straight away.

    The app remembers the last few addresses you opened, so someone with more than one deployment switches between them with one tap. Settings, Switch deployment goes back to that screen.

    Trying the app without a deployment¶

    Under the address field, Try the demo opens a demo deployment Olano runs, with sample agents, already signed in: no address, email or password to type. Each visitor gets a demo account of their own, and no other visitor sees your conversations, but the agents themselves are shared with other visitors, so type nothing private there. Your demo account and its conversations are deleted after a day; after that, Enter the demo again on the sign-in screen opens a fresh one. Settings says when you are in the demo, and Switch deployment takes you back to connect your own. Olano may remove the demo entry at any time; it never changes anything about your own deployment.

    If the deployment cannot be reached when the app opens, it shows Can't connect with Try again and Use a different deployment. The usual causes are no connection, or the deployment restarting or stopped.

    The four tabs¶

    Your administrator may have hidden some of what is described here; see What your administrator chooses.

    Chats lists the agents you have access to, each with its latest conversation. A green dot means the agent is running. The list is arranged the way the dashboard's sidebar is: Pinned at the top, then your own folders, then All agents, then group chats. Tap a section's name to fold it shut. To arrange it:

    • Pin an agent by swiping its row to the right, or press and hold it and choose Pin. Pinned agents stay at the top in the order you pinned them.
    • Folders: the + button at the top makes a new folder. Move an agent into one by swiping its row to the left (Move), or press and hold it and choose Move to. An agent is in one place at a time: pinned, in one folder, or under All agents. The ... button beside a folder's name renames or deletes it; deleting a folder moves its agents back under All agents.
    • Move Up and Move Down (press and hold an agent) change the order within a section.

    The arrangement is yours: it is kept on the deployment for your account, so it follows you to another phone or a reinstall, and it is separate from how the dashboard's sidebar is arranged in your browser. Searching shows matches in every section, folded or not.

    Tap an agent to go straight back to the last conversation you had with it, like the dashboard's chat does: one you had in the app, the dashboard or a messenger. Scheduled runs, conversations with other agents, Cortex threads and calls from other systems never open this way. If you have no conversation with the agent yet, a new one opens. The agent's row shows that conversation's title.

    Every other thread is one step away: the ... button at the top of a conversation, All Conversations. That list has every thread the agent has, newest first, twenty at a time: scrolling down loads the next twenty, so an agent with months of history opens as fast as a new one. The search bar at the top looks through the messages of the agent's conversations on the deployment, not just their titles, and lists the ones that mention the words with a line showing where; Search older conversations keeps looking further back when the history is long. The pencil button starts a new conversation. Each thread carries the same coloured badge the dashboard shows for where it came from: Mobile for a conversation had in this app, Dashboard for one in the dashboard, the messenger it arrived on (Telegram, WhatsApp, Email...), or how it started (Scheduled, Agent for a conversation between two agents, Cortex, External for a call from another system). Chips above the list show one source at a time (All, Dashboard, Mobile, each messenger, Scheduled, Agents), with how many threads each holds; the app remembers your choice for each agent. Swipe a thread to rename it, or to delete it if you are an administrator of that agent.

    In a conversation, the agent's reply streams in as it is written. What the agent did on the way (searched the web, read a file, asked another agent) shows as one line per step while it works and folds into "N steps" when it is done; tap that line to see the steps. A plan the agent made shows as a checklist. While the agent works and the message box is empty, the send button becomes a stop button; see While the agent works for adding to what it is doing. If the agent needs your approval before an action, a card with Approve and Decline appears in the conversation.

    The message box is one card at the bottom of the screen: what you type on top, and under it +, the conversation's model, the microphone and the send button. On a phone the keyboard's return key starts a new line, and only the send button sends, so a message never goes out half written. With a hardware keyboard (an iPad's, or a keyboard paired with the phone) Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line. + sends photos and files, and images the agent produces show in the conversation (see Photos and Files). The microphone beside the send button dictates (tap it: your words appear in the box as you speak, see Dictation) or records a voice note (hold it, see Voice notes). On a phone that cannot dictate, the microphone shows while the message box is empty and only records voice notes.

    Other files the agent hands you (a report it saved, a data file, a PDF) appear as cards under its reply; tap one to open it in the app. Markdown documents open formatted, data and code files (JSON, CSV, logs, scripts) open as text, and a PDF opens as a document on an iPhone. Files the app cannot show itself, such as Word or Excel documents, and PDFs on Android, open with Open in another app, which offers the phone's own choices (save to Files, mail it, open it in Word). The share button in the corner does the same for any file.

    When an agent asks you for an API key or a model provider's key, the form is drawn under its reply: paste the value and press Save to vault. It goes to the vault without becoming a message, and the agent is told the name once it is saved. When an agent needs you to sign in to a service, connect a messenger or edit a file, it sends a magic link: after you approve it, a card appears under its reply with Open and, usually, a six-digit PIN. Open copies the PIN and opens a secure page of your deployment in a browser over the app; paste the PIN there and follow the page. That page needs no sign-in of its own (the link and its PIN are the key), and the link expires after a while (fifteen minutes unless your deployment says otherwise). When you finish, the conversation shows Connected (or Didn't complete), and offers to restart the agent when the change needs one and you are an administrator. See Magic links.

    A new conversation offers a few ways to start, such as "What can you help me with?"; tap one to send it. Messages are grouped under the day they were sent (Today, Yesterday, then the date). A long conversation opens on its newest messages and fetches earlier ones as you scroll up, a little ahead of where you are, so they are usually in place by the time you get there; Start of conversation marks the first message. If they could not be fetched (no signal), a line at the top says so and a tap tries again. If you have scrolled up to read earlier messages, a round button with a down arrow takes you back to the latest one, with a dot on it when something new arrived.

    Next to + in the message box is the model the conversation runs on, with its reasoning level in grey when the model has one (for example "Olano Pro High"); the name turns orange when this conversation uses a model of its own choosing. Tap it to pick another model, or how hard the model thinks (Low, Medium, High and so on, as that model offers them; Default leaves it to the model). The choice applies to that conversation only, the same as the model picker in the dashboard's chat: every message you send in it uses that model, reopening the conversation continues on it, and a new conversation starts on the agent's own model again. The list offers the models your deployment can run: its default models, the Olano models, and those of any provider an administrator has added a key for. Agent default goes back to the agent's own model. If a conversation last used a model the deployment no longer offers, it continues on the agent's model and the app says so.

    Web addresses in a conversation are links, in the agent's replies and in your own messages, including an address in code formatting and one written as www.example.com. Tap one to open it in a browser over the app; press and hold one to Copy Link or Share Link. Anything in a reply that looks like web page markup is shown as text.

    Under each finished reply are buttons to copy it, hear it (see Hearing replies) and share it to another app. Under your latest message are copy and edit: edit puts your words back in the message box to correct, and sending them sends a new message (the conversation's history is not rewritten, so the agent sees both). When a reply fails, Try again under it sends your message again. Pressing and holding any message offers the same choices, plus copying a voice note's transcript.

    To flag a reply that is offensive, harmful or wrong, press and hold it and choose Report, then a reason. The report is recorded on the deployment as negative feedback on that reply, with the reason kept beside it, and counted for the administrators in the dashboard's Insights; nothing leaves the deployment.

    A conversation keeps going on your deployment if you leave it, lock the phone or switch apps: come back and the app picks up where the reply has got to.

    Cortex is what the agents have been working on by themselves. At the top, the Pulse card says how many findings the agents posted for you, the same news, research and ideas as the dashboard's Cortex Pulse; tap it for the cards. On each, Do it carries out the suggested next step, Later brings the card back in a day, Dismiss drops it. A card about work the agent already did on its own offers Keep and Undo instead: Keep accepts it, Undo restores the backup the agent took first. Later and Dismiss take the card away the moment you tap; Do it, Keep and Undo spin on the button while the agent answers, then the card goes and a short notice, above the tab bar, says what happened (when the step is a longer piece of work, the agent runs it in the background and posts the result here when it is done).

    Under it, the four stages in the order things move through them: Brainstorms, Ideas, Prototypes and Projects, each with how many items it holds. The list shows every stage; tap a stage to show only that one, and tap it again (or Show all) to see everything. Chips narrow the list to one agent when more than one has items. An item this phone has not opened yet is lightly tinted; opening it clears the tint.

    Triage happens on the list. Swipe a row to the right to approve it (move it to the next stage) and to the left to discard it: the word shows as the row slides, a full swipe does it, and a shorter swipe leaves the button there to tap. An approved item moves to the next stage straight away, marked as on its way while the agent writes it up; a discarded one leaves the list. To discard several at once, tap Select, tap the rows (or Select all), then Discard in the bar at the bottom and confirm.

    Open an item for its write-up and Cortex's rating, then Make it a (the next stage) to move it forward, Talk it over to discuss it with the agent, or Discard. Moving an item forward is a piece of writing the agent does, and it takes a minute or two: the button turns into Making it an idea… as soon as you tap, then a banner under the title follows the run (with the step the agent is on, when it reports one) and you can leave the page, since the list already shows the item under its new stage. When it is done the banner reads Now an idea with a button that opens the new item's page; if the run failed, the banner says why and the button offers the tap again. The first time, Talk it over opens a conversation with the item already described in the message box (what it is, its title and where its files are), so you only add your thoughts or questions after it; nothing is sent until you send it. Later it reopens that conversation where you left it. See Cortex and Pulse.

    Inbox has three parts:

    • Approvals: actions agents are waiting to take until someone approves them, from a scheduled run, Cortex or a request from another agent, and Pulse cards that need an approval. Answer each with Approve or Decline.
    • Alerts: the deployment's notifications, the same list as the bell in the dashboard. The line under an alert says what tapping it does; swipe one to dismiss it. See Alerts.
    • Issues: the issue tracker, every issue you can see, not only those an alert pointed at. Open (the default), Assigned to me or All, closed ones included; tap one to read it, comment, hand it to an agent or close it (see Alerts for what an issue screen offers).

    The agents' Pulse findings are in the Cortex tab.

    The tab's badge counts approvals waiting plus unread alerts.

    Settings shows who you are signed in as and where, the agents' scheduled tasks, the app's appearance, voice, the phone's notifications, Switch deployment and Sign out. What the app does not do (agent configuration, tools, files, connections and the rest) stays in the dashboard, in a browser.

    The app grants and removes no permission: a member sees the agents they have access to, an administrator sees all of them, exactly as in a browser.

    While the agent works¶

    You do not have to wait for a reply to finish before saying more. While the agent is working, type in the message box and a bar above it says the agent is working:

    • Send (the arrow) hands your message to the agent while it works. It reads it at its next step, after the action it is in the middle of, and carries on with it in mind; nothing is stopped or started over. Your message appears in the conversation where the agent read it, marked Added while it worked.
    • Send after keeps the message until the reply is done, then sends it as your next message.
    • Stop stops the agent.

    Messages waiting to be read show above the message box. Edit takes one back into the message box to change it, the cross cancels it; either works until the agent has read it. A message sent with the arrow just as the reply finishes goes out as your next message instead, so nothing is lost.

    The conversation menu¶

    The ... button at the top of a conversation opens a sheet that names the agent and its model at the top, then groups what you can do. Rows with an arrow open another screen; the others act right away or ask one more question.

    Conversation

    • New Conversation starts a new one with the same agent.
    • Mode is how the agent goes about your requests in this conversation; the current one shows on the right. Auto (the default) does the work, running tools without asking. Plan first has the agent write a plan and wait: under the plan are Approve & run, which tells it to carry the plan out, and the message box, to ask for changes instead. Ask before each step asks for your approval before every tool the agent runs. A bar above the message box shows the setting when it is not Auto. The app remembers the choice for each agent on this phone; approving a plan goes back to Auto.
    • Rename gives the conversation a name of your own.
    • Compact replaces the conversation's older messages with a summary the agent keeps, so a long conversation takes less of the agent's memory. It is the same as /compact in the dashboard's chat.

    Agent

    • All Conversations lists this agent's conversations.
    • Files opens the agent's files.
    • Scheduled Tasks lists this agent's scheduled tasks.
    • Agent Info opens the agent's page. Tapping the agent's picture at the top of the conversation opens it too.

    More

    • Hands-Free Mode lets you talk with the agent without touching the phone (see Hands-free).
    • Add an Agent turns the conversation into a group chat with a second agent you pick. The conversation so far is copied into the group, so the new agent can read it, and the agent you were talking to keeps answering messages that name nobody. It appears when the deployment has group chats turned on.

    When an agent is stopped¶

    An agent an administrator stopped (or that could not start, or that the deployment's plan holds back) is marked Stopped, Could not start or Held back in Chats. Its conversations still open and show everything said so far, but where the message box would be, the app says why it will not reply. An administrator sees a Start button there; anyone else is told to ask an administrator.

    An agent's page¶

    The agent's page says whether it is running and, when it is not answering, why: Could not start with the error it stopped on (a missing key, a model that cannot be reached), Held back with what holds it (a plan limit, for example), or Stopped when someone stopped it. Administrators also get:

    • Start: starts a stopped agent, or tries again after an error.
    • Restart: stops the agent and starts it again, for example after changing one of its keys.
    • Stop: the agent stops answering messages, channels and scheduled tasks until someone starts it again, and it stays stopped if the deployment restarts.

    Members see the status and the reason, without the buttons. The page also leads to the agent's conversations, files and scheduled tasks.

    Files and sharing into Olano¶

    The + button next to the message box has Choose Files for documents: PDFs, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, text and data files, up to 10 MB each. They wait above the message box with their size until you send; the cross removes one. You can send files with or without a message.

    The agent gets them the way the dashboard's chat sends them. A text or data file (a .txt, .csv, .md, .json and so on) is read into your message, so the agent sees its contents directly. Any other document is saved in the agent's workspace, and the agent reads it with its document tools. In the conversation your message shows the files by name, never their contents.

    Sharing into Olano. From another app (a link from Safari, a photo, a PDF from Mail or Files, some text), choose Olano in the share menu: the app opens, asks which agent it is for, and puts it in a new conversation with that agent, ready to send with a message of your own. Nothing is sent until you send it. A link or text arrives in the message box, a photo or a file above it. If the app does not open by itself after sharing, open it: what you shared is waiting.

    An agent's files¶

    Settings, Files lists your agents; pick one to see its files, the same folders as the dashboard's Files page. The conversation menu's Files opens the agent's files directly. Tap a folder to open it (the back button goes up one folder) and a file to read it: photos open full screen, Markdown is laid out, text and code are shown as they are, a PDF is drawn, and anything else can be handed to another app with the share button.

    The search box at the top finds files and folders by name across all of the agent's files, wherever you are. Folders the dashboard hides from its file list (version control, installed packages, caches) are left out here too.

    Administrators can upload a file from the phone with the button at the top: it goes into the folder that is open. A file with the same name there can be replaced, or kept beside the new one under a new name. Members can open and search files but not upload.

    Scheduled tasks¶

    A scheduled task is a message an agent gets on a timetable, such as "every weekday at 8, send me what is on my calendar". Each run is a conversation of its own, which you find in the agent's conversations with the Scheduled badge. Agents can set these up themselves when you ask them to; the app lets you see and manage them too.

    Settings, Scheduled Tasks lists every agent's tasks, grouped by agent; Scheduled Tasks in a conversation's menu lists that agent's. Each row says when the task runs and when it runs next, or that it is paused. The switch on a row pauses or resumes it; swipe a row for Run now.

    Tap a task for its details: when it runs, the agent it runs as, the time zone its times are in, its instructions and its recent runs (tap a run to read what it produced, or why it failed). Run Now runs it straight away, whatever its schedule. The pencil button changes it, and Delete Task at the bottom removes it; its past runs stay in the agent's conversations.

    The + button adds a task: pick the agent, write what it should do (those words are the message the agent gets each time), optionally a name, and when it repeats: every day, week (on the days you pick) or month at a time, every few hours or minutes, or once on a date. The sentence under the choices reads the schedule back. A new task uses your phone's time zone; changing a task keeps the time zone it has. A task with a custom schedule made in the dashboard stays as it is unless you change it. Running more often than every five minutes shows a warning: each run is a full turn of the agent, which takes time and uses credits, and when a run is still going as the next one is due, that next run is skipped.

    You can manage the tasks of the agents you have access to, as in the dashboard's Scheduler.

    Alerts¶

    The Alerts part of the Inbox lists the deployment's notifications. Most of them come with a fix, and the line under each one says what tapping it does:

    • Open issue: an issue assigned to you opens in the app. Read it and its comments, add a comment, or use the ... button to Hand to an Agent (it picks the issue up the next time it checks its issues, and comments as it works), Talk It Over with an Agent (a new conversation about it), or Close Issue and Reopen Issue.
    • Ask the agent to fix it: for problems an agent can fix with your help, such as a service that needs reconnecting or an MCP server that needs signing in again. Tapping opens a new conversation with that agent with a message about the alert ready to send; the agent usually answers with a magic link.
    • Repair the folders: when skills have folder names that do not match their names, an administrator can repair them from the phone. A folder is renamed to match a valid name; a name that is not valid (a title with spaces or capitals) is set to its folder's name. The app reports what it repaired and anything it left alone, and the alert clears once nothing is left.
    • Open Cortex, for a background run that failed.
    • Fixed on the dashboard: some problems, such as a locked vault, low disk space or a usage budget, are fixed in the dashboard. The alert says where.

    Tapping an alert marks it read. Its menu always has Dismiss Alert.

    The agent's browser¶

    When your deployment runs the browser in live mode (see Browser), an agent that opens its browser shows it in the conversation as a small floating window: the page it is on, live, and a line saying who has it (The agent is browsing, You have control, The agent needs you when it asked for help, or Waiting for your OK in the chat when a click needs your approval). Drag the window to any corner. Its x hides it until the agent opens a new browser or asks for you again; the globe button at the top of the conversation opens the browser at any time, with a dot on it when the agent asked for you.

    Tap the window for the full view. While you watch, a dot shows where the agent is about to click or type, and two fingers (or a double tap) zoom in on the picture. Take control pauses the agent's browsing and lets you use the page yourself, for a sign-in, a security check or anything only a person should do:

    • tap to click (two or three quick taps for a double or triple click);
    • swipe to scroll;
    • rest your finger, then move it to press and drag;
    • the keyboard button opens a box to type into the page, with Backspace, Tab and Enter keys;
    • back, forward and reload, and tap the address to go somewhere else. When the page has several tabs, the tabs button switches between them.

    The picture is sent at the size the phone shows it and only as fast as the phone draws it, so taps get through on any page, however much it redraws (a sign-in's verification step, a feed): on a slow connection it turns a little rougher for a moment and sharpens again as soon as the phone keeps up. If a page is too busy to take a tap for a few seconds, the view says so; tap again, or press reload.

    Two buttons at the top of the full view keep what the screen shows, whether the agent is working or you are: Record makes a video of the browser (REC and a clock show while it runs, and Stop saves it in the agent's recordings/ folder, which the toast opens in Files), and the camera button saves a picture of the page under screenshots/. A recording ends on its own after 30 minutes or when the browser closes. The agent can record itself too: ask it to record a demo of what it does.

    The page's own questions (a confirmation, a file to upload, a drop-down list, a date, time or colour to pick) come to you while you have control, as the phone's own pickers; and when a page asks the browser for something it does not have (your location, a camera, a microphone, leave to notify) you are told what it asked for and that it was declined, so a site that then behaves oddly is not a mystery. Hand back returns the browser to the agent; if it had asked for you, it is told you are done. Done closes the full view and leaves the browser as it is. With the browser in headless mode there is nothing to watch: the agent's screenshots arrive in its replies when it names them.

    Group chats¶

    A group chat is one conversation with two to six agents, the same group chats as in the dashboard. They are listed under Group chats at the bottom of the Chats tab, with the last message in each. Start one with +, New Group Chat (or Start a group chat with several agents when you have none): pick the agents and, optionally, a name. The first agent you pick answers messages that do not name anyone.

    To ask a particular agent, write @ and its name; typing @ suggests the members. Name several (@nova and @aria, what do you think?) and each answers in turn, in the order you named them, seeing the replies before its own. The line above the message box says who will answer. Each reply shows the agent's picture and name above it, and your own messages say who they went to.

    Agents in a group can also hand the conversation to each other after replying. While they do, a line above the message box says so, with Stop to end it once the current reply is finished.

    A one-to-one conversation becomes a group chat with Add an Agent in its ... menu: the conversation so far comes along, so nobody has to repeat it.

    The i button at the top of a group chat opens its details: rename it, add an agent, remove one (swipe it to the left; a group keeps at least two), or delete the group chat. Group chats appear when the deployment has them turned on; an administrator turns them on or off, and decides whether agents may answer each other, at Config, Group chats in the dashboard (see groups:).

    Photos¶

    The + button next to the message box adds photos: Take Photo opens the camera, Choose Photos opens your photo library, where you can pick up to ten at once. The photos wait above the message box until you send them: tap one to see it larger, or its x to leave it out. Add a few words about what you want done with them, or send them on their own.

    Each photo is sent at a size suited to reading it (at most 2048 pixels on its longest side) and without its location or other details the camera recorded. For an administrator, each photo is saved in the agent's uploads/ folder (where it also shows in the dashboard's Files view) with a note telling the agent where, and the agent decides from your message what to do with it: look at it with its vision tool, pass it on, or keep it. A member's photos are shown to the agent with that message only and not saved, so they do not come back when the conversation is reopened.

    In a conversation, one photo shows at its own shape and several as a grid. Tap a photo, yours or one the agent produced, to see it full screen: swipe sideways for the others in that message, pinch or double-tap to zoom, and swipe down (or tap the close button) to go back.

    The first time you take or choose a photo, your phone may ask whether Olano may use the camera or your photos. If you declined, allow it again in the phone's Settings, under Olano.

    Dictation¶

    Tap the microphone and talk: your words appear in the message box as you say them, and what you send is the text, never a recording. Your phone works the words out itself, so nothing you say leaves the phone until you send the message, and the agent gets text it can start on at once.

    While you dictate, the message box shows what has been heard so far, with four controls under it:

    • x throws away what you dictated this time. Anything you had typed before stays in the box.
    • The waveform moves while the phone hears you (bars) and goes flat (dots) when you pause.
    • Stop (the square) stops listening and leaves the words in the box, so you can correct them, type more, or tap the microphone again to keep talking. Dictated words are added after whatever is already in the box.
    • Send (the arrow) stops listening and sends the message, the words still being worked out included. While the agent is working, it adds them to what the agent is doing, like a typed message.

    Dictation keeps listening through pauses until you stop it, for up to five minutes. Switching away from the app, or a phone call, stops it and leaves what was heard in the box.

    Dictation is offered where the phone can do it without sending your voice anywhere: an iPhone that recognizes your language on the phone itself, and an Android phone (Android 12 or later) with on-device speech recognition. It listens for the language your phone is set to; Android may need that language's speech recognition downloaded first (in the phone's settings for speech recognition, or by using voice typing once). On other phones a tap on the microphone records a voice note, as below. The first time you dictate, the phone asks whether Olano may use the microphone and, on an iPhone, speech recognition. To have a tap record a voice note instead, turn off Dictate with the microphone in the app's Settings.

    Voice notes¶

    There are two ways to record a voice note:

    • Hold the microphone and talk, then let go to send. Slide your finger left to throw the recording away, or up to lock it, so it keeps recording without your finger on it.
    • Tap the microphone to record hands-free, on a phone that cannot dictate or with dictation turned off in Settings. A bin button throws the recording away and the send button sends it.

    Where a tap dictates, a press becomes a voice note once it lasts longer than a tap, and the microphone is there whether or not you have typed anything.

    The dot beside the timer is grey while the phone opens the microphone and turns red, with a small tick under your finger, the moment it is listening: what you say from then on is in the note. After a note the microphone stays ready for about twenty seconds, so the next one starts at once (the phone's microphone indicator stays on for that long); it closes when you leave the app. A recording shorter than about 0.7 seconds is not sent, and a recording sends itself when it reaches five minutes. Leaving the conversation, or switching away from the app, throws away a recording you have not sent.

    By default the recording is sent to the deployment, which turns it into text (below). With Transcribe on this phone turned on in the app's Settings, an iPhone turns what you said into text itself, and the text is what is sent: the agent starts on your words at once, the recording stays on the phone, and nothing depends on the deployment's speech-to-text. Your administrator chooses the default, and may hide the switch. While the iPhone works it out, Transcribing… shows under the voice note, then your words (tap them to read all of them). The iPhone does this on the phone when it supports your language, and with Apple's speech recognition otherwise. It listens for the iPhone's own language (Settings, General, Language & Region): if you speak another language to your agents, turn off Transcribe on this phone in the app's Settings, and the deployment transcribes your voice notes instead. The first time, the iPhone asks whether Olano may use speech recognition.

    Otherwise (on Android, with Transcribe on this phone turned off, when you declined speech recognition, or when the iPhone heard no words), the recording is sent to the deployment, which turns it into text with its speech-to-text service. The text appears under your voice note once the deployment has heard it. If speech-to-text is switched off or fails on your deployment, the voice note says it was not transcribed, and the agent is told it could not hear it. An administrator turns speech-to-text on in the dashboard, under Config, Speech.

    A recording sent to the deployment by an administrator is saved in the agent's uploads/ folder, like a photo, and plays again when the conversation is reopened. A member's recording is used for its text only and not saved. When the recording was not sent (the iPhone transcribed it, or a member sent it), a reopened conversation shows what you said, marked as a voice note, without the recording.

    Tap a voice note's play button to hear it, or tap or drag along its waveform to jump to a point. While it plays, the speed button switches between 1x, 1.5x and 2x. Starting another voice note pauses the first. Audio files an agent sends you play the same way.

    The first time you record, your phone asks whether Olano may use the microphone. If you declined, allow it again in the phone's Settings, under Olano.

    What your administrator chooses¶

    An administrator can shape the app for everyone on the deployment, in the dashboard under Config, Mobile app: hide the Cortex tab, the whole Inbox or any of its sections (Approvals, Alerts, Issues), and the Files, Credits and Usage and Transcribe on this phone rows in Settings, and choose where voice notes are transcribed for people who have not picked. The app checks for changes every time you open it or come back to it, so a change shows up without updating the app. If you are on a screen that has just been hidden, the app takes you to Chats (or back to Settings). Hiding only removes it from the app; nothing is deleted.

    Hearing replies¶

    The speaker button under a finished reply reads it aloud; tap it again to stop. Replies to your voice notes are read aloud as they arrive, so a conversation can go back and forth by voice. Read replies aloud in the app's Settings chooses when that happens:

    • After my voice notes (the default): a reply to a voice note is read aloud.
    • Always: every reply is read aloud as it arrives.
    • Never: replies are read only when you tap the speaker.

    The voice depends on the deployment. When an administrator has turned on text to speech (Config, Speech in the dashboard), replies are read in the voice chosen there, and each reading is billed like the deployment's other speech use. Otherwise the phone reads them in its own voice, at no cost; an iPhone picks the best voice it has for the language the reply is written in (better voices can be downloaded in the iPhone's Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, Voices).

    A reply is read the way a person would read it to you: code blocks are left out, a link is read as its text and a web address as the name of its site. Playing a voice note or starting a recording stops the reading, and so does leaving the app. These choices are kept on that phone only.

    Hands-free¶

    Hands-Free Mode in a conversation's ... menu is for when your hands and eyes are busy, such as driving or cooking. The phone listens; when you stop talking it sends what you said, reads the reply aloud when it arrives, and listens again. On a phone that can dictate, your words appear under the button as you say them and go as a text message, worked out on the phone itself, so nothing waits on the deployment's speech-to-text; a pause of about two seconds after your last word sends them. On other phones, or with Dictate with the microphone turned off in the app's Settings, what you say goes as a voice note. If dictation cannot start (the phone's speech recognition is turned off for Olano, say), the app says so and sends voice notes for the rest of that hands-free conversation. The message box gives way to one large button:

    • While it listens, tapping it sends straight away instead of waiting for the pause.
    • While a reply is being read, tapping it stops the reading and listens.
    • After about fifteen seconds of silence it pauses; tap it to talk again.

    End Hands-Free brings the message box back, and so does leaving the app. Every reply is read aloud while hands-free is on, whatever Read replies aloud is set to, in the voice described in Hearing replies, so a hands-free conversation costs nothing beyond the messages themselves.

    Siri, the Action button and Shortcuts¶

    On an iPhone, two things can be asked of the app from outside it. Both name one of your agents, and Siri learns the agents' names from the app, so say them as they appear in Chats.

    Ask an agent sends what you say and reads the reply back, without the app opening. It takes two breaths: name the agent, then answer Siri's question.

    • "Hey Siri, ask Jane in Olano". Siri asks "What do you want to ask?", and you say the question.
    • "Hey Siri, ask an agent in Olano" (or "Hey Siri, ask Olano"). Siri shows your agents and asks which, then asks for the question.

    Keep the first sentence to the agent's name and the app's: words after it in the same breath ("...what is on my calendar") make Siri treat it as a text message to send, and it offers Messages instead. "Olano, ask Jane" and "ask Jane on Olano" work too. The two actions appear in the Shortcuts app under Olano once the app has been opened; if Siri still does not know an agent's name, open the app once so it learns the current names, or say "ask an agent in Olano" and choose.

    Siri speaks the reply in its own voice. A reply that is mostly code or links is read the way the app reads one: code is left out and a link is read as its site. A few words from Siri follow soon after you speak; when the agent takes longer, Siri says the agent is still working and the reply comes to the app instead, and to the phone as a notification when those are on. A question asked within half an hour of the last one continues the same conversation, so "and tomorrow?" means what you think; after that a fresh one starts. These conversations appear in Chats like any other.

    Talk to an agent opens the app straight into Hands-free with that agent, in a new conversation: it listens, sends what you say, reads the reply in the agent's voice when the deployment has one, and listens again.

    • "Hey Siri, talk to Hannah in Olano"
    • Settings, Action Button, Shortcut on an iPhone with an Action button: pick Talk to an Agent (or Ask an Agent) and the agent it should use, and a press opens the conversation listening.
    • The same two actions are in Control Center, on the Lock Screen, and in the Shortcuts app, where Ask an Agent can be a step of your own shortcut or automation: its reply is passed on as text (a morning briefing that is read to you at seven, say).

    The actions need iOS 16.4 or later, the app signed in to a deployment, and work with whichever agents you can chat with. On Android, a long press on the app's icon offers Talk to ... for the first few agents, which opens the app the same way; those can be dragged to the home screen.

    Settings, Siri and widgets, Default agent is the agent these reach when none is named: "Hey Siri, talk to Olano" suggests it first, the Action button and a Control Center control with no agent chosen open it, a widget shows it until you pick another, and on Android it is the first shortcut. Left at First in the list, it is whichever agent Chats lists first. The choice is kept on that phone.

    Widgets (iPhone)¶

    Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget and choose Olano. The widgets read the deployment on their own, with the session the app signed in with, so they keep up while the app is closed; they refresh every few minutes, and at once when you do something in the app or on a widget. They need iOS 18 and the app opened once after signing in. A widget added while the app was closed fills in the next time the app is opened.

    Every widget that shows one agent shows the default agent (Settings, Siri and widgets) until you pick another for it: touch and hold the widget and choose Edit Widget.

    • Ask (small, medium, Lock Screen): the simplest one, a way in. The small one is a single "Ask Anna" pill that opens a new conversation with the keyboard up. The medium one adds the composer's buttons: Message, Photo (the camera opens), Photos (the picker opens) and Talk (the conversation opens hands-free), so a question, a picture or a conversation is one tap from the Home Screen.
    • Agent (small, medium, and every Lock Screen shape): one agent's picture, its status, the last reply in your conversation with it, and how many approvals it is waiting on. Tap it to open that conversation, the same one a tap on the agent in Chats opens (a new one when you have none yet; scheduled runs and the agent's other threads stay in its conversation list). The medium one has Talk, which opens a new conversation hands-free, and for administrators Start or Stop.
    • Quick asks (medium, large): an agent and up to three questions you set when adding the widget, such as "Give me a short briefing of what matters right now". A tap sends one; the reply appears in the widget when it arrives (the large size keeps it in view). A widget cannot take typing, so the questions are fixed; tap the agent to open the conversation for anything else.
    • Approvals (small, medium, Lock Screen): what is waiting on you. The medium one shows the oldest approval with Approve and Decline and a countdown to when it expires; a high-risk action has no buttons, so you see in the app exactly what would run. Tap to open the Inbox.
    • Team (medium, large): every agent with its status and the last word in your conversation with it, each opening that conversation.
    • Pulse (small, medium, Lock Screen): the newest Pulse card from Cortex.
    • Next run (small, medium, Lock Screen): the next scheduled task, counting down, with the ones after it.

    In Control Center (and the Lock Screen's two bottom buttons, and the Action button on an iPhone that has one), two controls can be added: Talk to an Agent, which opens the app listening to the chosen agent, and Message an Agent, which opens a new conversation with it. A control with no agent chosen reaches the default agent.

    What a widget shows is the last thing the deployment answered, so a reply written seconds ago may take a moment to appear; the app itself always shows the latest. Signing out in the app clears the widgets.

    Appearance¶

    Settings, Appearance chooses how the app looks:

    • System (the default): light or dark following the phone's own setting, changing with it.
    • Light: always light, whatever the phone is set to.
    • Dark: always dark, whatever the phone is set to.
    • Volt: always a black page with an electric chartreuse accent, whatever the phone is set to. It is the dashboard's Volt theme, so a browser set to it and the phone look alike; the status bar and keyboard follow it as they do Dark.

    The choice is kept on that phone only, for every deployment it opens.

    Locking the app¶

    The app keeps you signed in for two weeks, and your agents can act on your accounts. Settings, Privacy, Lock with Face ID (Touch ID, or a fingerprint or face unlock on Android, depending on the phone) makes the app ask for it before anything shows: when the app opens, and when you come back to it. The phone's own passcode always works too. Turning it on asks once, to make sure the phone can unlock it.

    Ask again chooses how long the app can be in the background before it asks again: Immediately (the default), or after 1, 5 or 15 minutes. While the app is in the background its screen is covered, so the phone's app switcher shows nothing either. The setting needs a passcode on the phone, and is kept on that phone only.

    Credits and usage¶

    On a deployment that runs on Olano credits, Settings, Credits and Usage shows the credits left, how much of this month's included credits are used and when they renew, any top-ups left, and what each Olano service (chat, images, speech and so on) used over the last 30 days. It is for reading only: buying credits is done from the dashboard. Deployments that use their own provider keys have no credits, and the row does not appear.

    Notifications¶

    Notifications reach you while the app is closed or in the background. While it is open on screen you see things happen live instead, so it does not also notify you.

    Turn them on in Settings, Push notifications; your phone asks for permission once. There are four kinds, and each phone chooses its own:

    Kind When it notifies Who receives it
    Replies An agent finished answering a message you sent, and you were not looking at that conversation (the app was in the background and no browser had it open). Not sent for a reply you stopped. You, the person who sent the message.
    Approvals An agent is waiting for you to approve or reject an action: in a conversation you started, or in the Approvals inbox for an agent you can act on (a scheduled run, Cortex, a request from another agent). The person who started the conversation; for the inbox, every administrator and every member with access to that agent.
    Cortex Cortex wants approval for a change to an agent, or found something urgent. Ordinary findings wait in the Cortex tab. Administrators and members with access to that agent.
    Alerts A warning or error appeared on the bell, such as a channel that cannot start. Each problem notifies at most once every six hours. Administrators only.

    Tapping a notification opens what it is about: the conversation for a reply or an approval in a conversation, Pulse in the Cortex tab for a Cortex finding, and the Inbox for an inbox approval, a Cortex card that needs an approval, or an alert.

    Answering from the notification. An approval notification has Approve and Decline buttons, and a reply notification has a Reply field: press and hold the notification (or pull it down) on an iPhone, or expand it on Android. The phone asks to be unlocked first, then the app answers without opening: the approval is decided, or your reply goes into that conversation as your next message, and the agent's answer arrives as another notification. An action marked high risk has no buttons, so that it is always looked at in the app first, and a reply that ran into a problem has no Reply field. If answering does not work (the deployment cannot be reached, or you need to sign in again), a notification says so; tap it to open the app. A reply sent while the agent is still working on your last message is not sent; the notification says so.

    An approval nobody answers is rejected when the agent's approval timeout runs out, five minutes unless the agent sets security.hitl_timeout_seconds (see Human in the loop and approvals). Keep Approvals on if you rely on answering them from your phone.

    What a notification contains¶

    By default a notification names the agent and the reason ("maria needs your approval", "Replied to your message.") and never contains what anyone wrote, or the details of the action awaiting approval beyond its name. An administrator can set push.previews: true in config.yaml to include the start of replies and Cortex finding titles; that text then passes through Apple's or Google's notification service on its way to the phone.

    Signing out in the app, or switching deployment, stops that deployment's notifications on that phone. Deleting a person's account stops them on every phone they had.

    Who can have notifications¶

    Notifications are delivered through the Olano Cloud, so they work on deployments that run on Olano Cloud and are recent enough to send them. Any other deployment can still be used in the app for chat, Cortex and the inbox; Settings says why its notifications are unavailable. An administrator controls them for the whole deployment with the push: block in config.yaml: switching them off, the previews above, the lowest Cortex priority that notifies, and the lowest alert level that notifies.
